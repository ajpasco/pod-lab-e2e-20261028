## The defect

`list` writes its whole output to stdout at once. When the reader stops reading before the end
(`shelf list | head -1`, or `shelf list | true`, with a long list), the write fails with `EPIPE`
and the boundary has no handler for a write error on stdout, so Node's default applies: an unhandled
`error` event, a stack trace of about fourteen lines on stderr, and exit 1. The PRD was silent on a
reader that closes the pipe, and blueprint section 12 had excluded the case as a recorded default.
The founder chose the behaviour, and it is now REQ-02 c8.

## What to build

Handle a failed write to stdout in the boundary (TD-21). When the failure is a closed pipe (`EPIPE`),
the reader asked for less and got it: nothing goes on stderr and the exit code stays 0. Any other
failure to write stdout is still an unclassified failure under TD-11 (`shelf: unexpected error`, the
stack, exit 1). The blueprint fixes the mechanism as an `error` listener on `process.stdout`,
installed before `main` runs; the `process` global is allowed in the boundary and nowhere else.

What the reader then sees (REQ-02 c8): with a long shelf, `shelf list | head -1` prints its first
line, stderr is empty and shelf exits 0; `shelf.json` is untouched (REQ-02 c5). Every refusal and
every unclassified failure that is not a closed pipe is reported exactly as before.
